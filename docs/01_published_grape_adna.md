# 1. Published grape aDNA studies

This chapter summarises grape palaeogenomic papers whose abstracts or full texts were opened for these chapters. Titles without an abstract are listed only as titles. No unpublished laboratory counts are added. Public dataset accession numbers that we did not re-fetch in the sources opened for these chapters are not copied from other repositories.

## Studies whose abstracts or full texts were opened

Cappellini et al. (2010, full text, PMC2812422; doi:10.1007/s00114-009-0629-3) report “the first integrated investigation of both ancient DNA and proteins in archaeobotanical samples: medieval grape (*Vitis vinifera* L.) seeds, preserved by anoxic waterlogging,” from an early medieval (seventh–eighth century A.D.) Byzantine rural settlement in the Salento area (Lecce, Italy) and a late (fourteenth–fifteenth century A.D.) medieval site in York (England).

Ramos-Madrigal et al. (2019, abstract; doi:10.1038/s41477-019-0437-5) “present target-enriched genome-wide sequencing data from 28 archaeological grape seeds dating to the Iron Age, Roman era and medieval period.” The abstract says the archaeological samples “were closely related to western European cultivars used for winemaking today,” that seeds with identical genetic signatures were present at different Roman sites, and that “one seed dated to ~1100 CE was a genetic match to ‘Savagnin Blanc’, providing evidence for 900 years of uninterrupted vegetative propagation.”

Cohen et al. (2023, full text, PMC10151551; doi:10.1073/pnas.2213563120) “applied target-enriched genome-wide sequencing and radiocarbon dating” to grapevine pips from Late Antiquity settlements in the Negev Highlands. The abstract states that the analyses “revealed centuries long and continuous grape cultivation in the Southern Levant,” that “a pip dated to the eighth century CE was determined to likely be from a white grape, to date the oldest to be identified,” and that in a kinship analysis “another pip was found to be descendant from a modern Greek cultivar.”

Noraz et al. (2026, full text, PMC13013926; doi:10.1038/s41467-026-70166-z) “report genome-wide ancient DNA data from 49 archaeological grape pips spanning ~4000 years, from the Bronze Age to the Medieval period, primarily focusing on France.” The abstract says vegetative propagation, “evidenced by genetically identical clones across sites and centuries, emerged by the mid-Iron Age,” and that “one Medieval sample from Valenciennes is genetically identical to modern ‘Pinot Noir’, demonstrating clonal continuity over nearly 600 years.” That identity is the authors’ published claim; a same-clone report still needs the authentication and contamination checks in chapters 2 and 3, and the tissue-origin caution in the existing WORKFLOW (seed coat versus embryo remains **[待确认]** for *Vitis*).

Pérez-Jordà et al. (2026, abstract only; doi:10.1016/j.cub.2026.08.039) analysed “28 grapevine seeds from seven archaeological sites in the Iberian Peninsula and Sardinia.” The abstract says ancient DNA from the oldest seeds with domesticated-like morphology (from ∼1,000 BCE) in southwestern Spain “revealed nuclear and chloroplast genome signatures of Eastern Mediterranean cultivars,” and that later Iron Age Iberian seeds “showed genomic signatures suggesting hybridization between local wild grapevines and eastern-origin cultivars.” Only the opened abstract is used here.

Meiri and Bar-Oz (2024, full text, PMC11079635; doi:10.1016/j.tig.2024.02.003) is a fruit-crop paleogenomics review. It is cited as a review of the field, not as a new grape dataset.

Bolognesi et al. (2025, full text, PMC12540996; doi:10.1038/s41598-025-21743-7) tested extraction methods on ancient grape pips and reported that a soil-optimised reagent plus an aDNA-specific silica-binding step gave “higher yields and more consistent performance across sites compared to previous extraction methods” and improved library production, “particularly in challenging sites.” This is a recovery-methods paper, not a population-history result.

Malenica et al. (2011, abstract; doi:10.1007/s00114-011-0826-8), titled “Whole genome amplification and microsatellite genotyping of herbarium DNA revealed the identity of an ancient grapevine cultivar,” is an herbarium-grape identification study (abstract opened). It is not a shotgun palaeogenome.

## Titles only (no abstract opened for these chapters)

These records were title-matched, but no abstract was obtained, so their results are not taught:

- Wales et al. (2016), “The limits and potential of paleogenomic techniques for reconstructing grapevine domestication” (doi:10.1016/j.jas.2016.05.014).
- Inanli et al. (2026), “Grapevine cultivation at Cetamura del Chianti: multiproxy evidence for centuries of continuity from the Etruscans to the Romans” (doi:10.1016/j.jas.2026.106605).

## Morphometric companions (not sequence data)

Several opened abstracts describe grape-seed *shape*, not ancient genomes: Terral et al. (2010, doi:10.1093/aob/mcp298), Bonhomme et al. (2020, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0239863), Bouby et al. (2024, doi:10.1038/s41598-024-72692-6), and Ucchesu et al. (2024, doi:10.3390/plants13131836). They can support archaeological context; they are not a substitute for aDNA authentication.
